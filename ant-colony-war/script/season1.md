ANT COLONY WAR — SEASON 1
Narrator: Ant 250 (trap-jaw ant), first person. Voice: low, calm, cinematic.

EPISODE 1 — "WAKE"
Dirt. That's the first thing I felt. Cold dirt on my face, and a heartbeat that wasn't slowing down. I tried to move my legs, and all six answered at once, too fast, like they belonged to someone else. They told me later I was gone for two days. Dead. But I could hear everything: a beetle digging three tunnels over, a drop of water about to fall, the queen breathing somewhere deep below. I could smell fear in the walls, old fear, from ants who'd died here long before me. My jaws twitched open, then snapped shut so fast the air cracked. I'd never done that before. My name is 250. I'm two months old. And before this... I was nobody.

EPISODE 2 — "NUMBER 250"
Three hundred twenty ants in my colony. I was born 250th. Not first, not last, just... in the middle. The first ants born get names, stories, a place near the queen. The last ones get pity. The ones in the middle get nothing. In the nursery tunnels, nobody learned my name because I didn't have one. I carried crumbs. I followed lines. When the soldiers marched past, I stepped aside without being asked. I was good at being invisible. Too good. Sometimes I wondered if I'd disappear completely and nobody would even count the difference. Then one morning, out past the root wall, where young ants aren't supposed to go, I stepped in something that glowed.

EPISODE 3 — "THE GLOW"
It was green, kind of like light you could touch. It pulsed, slowly, like it was breathing. I should've run. Every instinct told me to run. But I just stood there, staring, because nothing in my whole life had ever looked back at me before. It crawled up my leg like it knew exactly where it was going. It was warm, then hot, then burning. Then it went inside. I felt it moving through me, through every segment, rewriting something I didn't understand. I remember the burning, and I remember falling. The dirt rushed up to meet me. The last thing I saw was the sky, and for the first time I wondered what was up there looking back.

EPISODE 4 — "INVISIBLE"
When I came back, the workers had already carried me to the waste chamber. Ants don't bury their dead, they just throw them out. Nobody stays to say goodbye. Nobody even checks twice. I woke up under the weight of a hundred bodies, old workers, broken soldiers, ants I used to walk beside. I pushed my way out, one leg at a time, and the green in my veins glowed brighter with every step. So I climbed out of a pile of bodies, walked back into the colony, and nobody noticed. Nobody even looked up. The same ants who threw me away two days ago walked right past me. One even bumped into me and said sorry, like I was a stranger. And that's when I realized being invisible might be my greatest weapon.

EPISODE 5 — "TESTING"
I started testing it in secret. A falling grain of sand? I could catch it before it hit the ground. A twig the soldiers needed four ants to cut? I snapped through it alone. And when I closed my eyes, I could smell every ant in the tunnels. All 319 of them. Plus something that didn't belong. Something from outside... and it was getting closer.

EPISODE 6 — "THE SCOUT"
Fire ants. A scout from the colony across the creek, hiding in our walls and counting us. I could have warned the queen, but who'd listen to number 250? So I followed him home that night. What I saw there wasn't a colony. It was an army. Thousands of them. And they were all facing our way.

EPISODE 7 — "THE FALL"
I ran home faster than any ant has ever run. My legs were a blur. I screamed it through every tunnel: they're coming. The guards laughed. A soldier pushed me into the dirt and said, "Go carry your crumbs, 250." So I went to the one ant I thought would listen. Captain Drax Venn, head of the gate guard. He listened to every word. He nodded. He told me he'd handle it. Then he walked to the outer gate... and opened it. I smelled it before I saw it: fire ant scent, all over him. He'd been working for them the whole time. By sunrise the walls were burning. I fought. I snapped through twelve of them, then twenty, until my jaws ached and the tunnels were slick with venom. It didn't matter. There were too many. Our queen was dragged out in chains, screaming my colony's name... and Drax stood beside the fire general, smiling. And I watched.

EPISODE 8 — "CHAINS"
They marched us across the creek on a bridge made of our own dead. Three hundred twenty of us had become one hundred and six. And Drax? They gave him a place in their army, the one trap-jaw with no chains. In the fire colony, we weren't ants anymore. We were tools. We dug their tunnels, fed their larvae, and slept in the cold chambers near the surface where the rain came in. Every morning a fire soldier named Krell Dukar walked down our line and picked one of us to make an example of. Every morning I kept my head down and let him pass. Every night I listened to Drax laughing with the fire soldiers above us. Because I'd learned something the night my colony fell: strength you show too early gets you killed. So I watched. I learned their scent signals. I learned which guards were lazy, which tunnels led to the queen, and which of their soldiers secretly hated each other. They thought I was just another slave. Good. Let them think that. And one day, Drax, I'm coming for you first.

EPISODE 9 — "THE SNAP"
The day it changed, Krell picked an old worker from my colony. The ant who used to feed me in the nursery, back when I was so small I couldn't carry my own crumb. He raised his stinger, and before I even decided to, I moved. One snap. Krell dropped, and the whole chamber went silent. Not one ant breathed. I waited for them to kill me. Instead, the fire queen's general stepped forward, looked at the body, then looked at me. "You," he said. "You fight for us now." Then he pointed at Drax. "Prove it." Drax backed away. He begged. He said we were brothers, that we came from the same dirt. I didn't hesitate. One snap. The fire soldiers cheered. My own colony stared at me like I was a monster. I should've said no. I should've stood with my own. But when they gave me Krell's armor and the fire soldiers stepped aside as I walked past... something in my blood purred. The green in my veins burned brighter than it ever had. The old worker whispered, "Thank you, 250." I didn't answer. I didn't even look back.

EPISODE 10 — "MY COLONY" (SEASON FINALE)
Fire ants rule through fear. Their venom burns for days, and their queen hasn't left her chamber in three years. But I was faster than their venom. Over the next weeks I climbed their ranks. I snapped the jaws of every soldier who challenged me, until no one challenged me anymore. They stopped calling me trap-jaw. They started calling me Lord Mandros. Then the general who made me a soldier started to fear me. So did the queen. One night she sent twenty of her best to kill me while I slept. They didn't know I don't really sleep anymore. I heard them three tunnels away. I let them get close. Close enough to see my eyes glow green in the dark. Then I moved. In the morning, I walked into the royal chamber alone, covered in red. The general was already dead at the door. The queen looked at me and whispered, "What are you?" I told her the truth. "I'm number 250. And this is my colony now." The whole army bowed. Thousands of them. Behind me, my own kind, the trap-jaws, were still in chains. The old worker pushed to the front and begged, "Free us. Please. You're one of us." I looked at him for a long time. Then I turned to my new soldiers and said, "Make them dig deeper." I could've freed them. I didn't. Not yet. Maybe not ever.
