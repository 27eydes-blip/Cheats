# Decisions log

Choices made while building unattended on `design-v2`, with the reason for each.

## Part A — For You feed

- **Where the tabs live:** the home page keeps the hero lead story, then a sticky **For You | Following** switch, then an infinite feed. The previous "Top stories" grid became part of For You (stories appear as large image cards inside the feed). Channel pages (`/?c=Politics` …) keep the grid layout.
- **What For You ranks:** recent top-level dispatches (7 days, newest 400) **and** stories (top 40). Logged-out and brand-new users have an empty interest profile, so the same ranker produces a trending + breaking mix.
- **Scoring** (`src/lib/ranking.ts`, pure and unit-tested): `freshness × engagement × personal`, with
  - engagement = log of recency-weighted activity: reactions/agree stances ×1, responses ×1.5, relays/quotes/shares ×2.5, views ×0.04; activity in the last 6h counts 3×, last 24h 1.5×;
  - freshness = 1/(ageHours+2)^1.25;
  - personal = interests (topic ×0.25, account ×0.35, tag ×0.15, clamped −0.85…+1.5) × social proof (+0.25 per followed account that engaged, max +1.0) × breaking (×1.6 for live stories or the story linked from the Breaking banner).
  - **Outrage guard:** when responses + disagreement exceed 2× positive engagement, the engagement is damped (down to ×0.35). Disagreement never adds score.
  - **Boost cap:** no item can score more than 8× its freshness baseline.
  - **Promoted items** never receive ranking help and always show a "Promoted" label. None exist yet; `posts.promoted` is the switch.
- **Variety:** at most 2 in a row from the same account or topic; every 6th slot pulls in the best item from a topic the viewer hasn't shown interest in ("Something new").
- **Interests** are stored per user as weights for topics, accounts and tags (clamped −4…+6). Signals: view +0.05, click +0.3, dwell ≥8s +0.4, react/agree +0.5, response +0.6, relay/share +0.7, vault +0.6, follow +2 (account), Show more +1.5, Not interested −1.5, onboarding beat +2.
- **Topics** are Politics, Markets, Sports, Tech, World, Culture, National (mapped from story sections; Business → Markets). Onboarding lists Politics, Markets, Sports, Tech, World first.
- **Tracking:** the feed records a view after 1s at ≥60% visible, dwell time when a card leaves, and clicks; batches go to a rate-limited server action every 10s and on page hide. Logged-out events are stored without a user id.
- **Migrations:** added a numbered, append-only migration runner (`src/lib/db/migrations.ts`, table `schema_migrations`). Migration 1 = interests, engagement_events, feed_dismissals, muted_topics, posts.promoted. Migration 2 = reactions table and users.is_private (needed by the ranker for later parts). Postgres versions appended to `supabase/schema.sql`.
- **Pagination:** offset cursor over the ranked list; the client de-duplicates, so a re-rank between pages can't show an item twice.
- **Muting:** topics are muted from any item's menu or in Settings → Your feed; account mutes reuse the existing mute.

## Part B — Sports teams and logos

- **No logo files are committed and nothing is scraped.** Logos come only from (1) a Control Room upload, where the admin must tick "we have a license or written permission", or (2) a licensed provider configured by environment variables. Otherwise every team shows a generated badge.
- **Provider adapter** (`src/lib/sports/logo-provider.ts`): `SPORTS_LOGO_PROVIDER` picks the adapter, `SPORTS_API_KEY` is the key (env only). Shipped adapters: `none` (default) and `manifest`, a generic "licensed JSON feed" adapter (`SPORTS_LOGO_MANIFEST_URL` returns `{ "nfl-kc": "https://…png" }`, called with the key as `Authorization: Bearer` and `X-API-Key`). I did **not** ship vendor-specific adapters I couldn't verify against a live contract; adding one is one class plus one line in `PROVIDERS`.
- **Caching:** each logo is fetched once, validated (PNG/WebP/JPEG/GIF only; **SVG refused** because it can carry script), re-encoded to PNG (≤512px, transparency kept), and stored under `UPLOAD_DIR/team-logos`. Status lives in the `teams` table. If the provider is down, the cached file keeps serving. Manual uploads always win and are never overwritten by a refresh. `npm run teams:refresh [-- --force]` re-fetches by hand; pages also warm missing logos in the background when a provider is configured.
- **Fallback badge:** a shield (US leagues) or crest (MLS/EPL) in the team's primary color, a secondary-color chevron at the base, and the abbreviation in the secondary color if it reaches 4.5:1 on the fill, else black or white (whichever is higher; one of them always clears 4.5:1). Tested for every seeded team. An outline appears automatically when the fill is close to the dark or light page background.
- **Teams seeded:** NFL 32, NBA 30, MLB 30 (Athletics listed as Sacramento), NHL 32 (incl. Utah Mammoth), MLS 30 (incl. San Diego FC), Premier League 2025–26 (20), and 46 major college football programs (SEC, Big Ten, Big 12, ACC power programs, Notre Dame, Boise State). Colors are the clubs' published primary/secondary values to the best of my knowledge; spot-check a few against brand guides before print use (noted in the data file).
- **Story tagging:** full team names match anywhere; bare nicknames ("Chiefs", "Huskies") match only in Sports stories, only when unique across all leagues, and case-sensitively (so "senators" in a politics story doesn't tag the Ottawa Senators: a test caught this). Shared nicknames like "Tigers" never tag on their own.
- **Where teams appear:** story cards and the hero (badges on the image), the story page header, the ticker (badges next to sports items), the ⌘K search overlay and /search (a Teams group), a new **/sports** directory (filter by league) and **/sports/[team]** pages listing tagged stories, and the Sports channel ("All teams").
- **Not built:** scoreboards, game pages and betting/picks pages, because the app has no scores or odds data source yet. `TeamLogo` (sizes sm/md/lg, lazy, auto-fallback) is ready for them. I did not add new sample stories about real teams, to avoid inventing news about real clubs; the existing fictional "Huskies win the national title" sample story gets tagged and shows how it works.

## Part C — WorldNet profiles

- **Vocabulary:** posts are **Dispatches**, replies **Responses** (button: "Respond"), reposts **Relays**, likes **Boosts** (the profile tab "Boosted" lists dispatches you reacted to; Part D's reactions are the Boost), bookmarks the **Vault**. Renamed across the interface, notifications and error messages. Code identifiers, URLs (`/bookmarks`, `/p/…`) and database names are unchanged, so links and data keep working. "Story", "Stance" and "Rundown" keep their names; they're WorldNet concepts, not Twitter ones.
- **Layout:** full-width banner (accent-colored gradient when there's no photo), a **left identity panel** (avatar, name, verified seal, @handle, Friends / Follows you / Private badges, bio, location, link, join date, Follow or Edit, Beat chips, Credibility Meter, Followers / Following / Friends tiles), and the content column on the right with pinned dispatches and tabs **Dispatches · Responses · Media · Boosted**, plus **Debates** (kept from the old byline page so no feature is lost). On phones the panel stacks above the tabs.
- **Beat:** up to 6 topics from a fixed list of 12, shown as colored chips (deeper colors in light mode for contrast).
- **Credibility Meter (0–100):** baseline 20; up to +40 for the share of dispatches with a source link (counts fully after ~10 dispatches, so one sourced post can't max it); +2 per sourced context note (max 10); +15 verified; +1 per ~5 weeks on WorldNet (max 10); −15 per dispatch removed by moderators (max −45). Levels: Building <40, Solid 40–69, Trusted 70–89, Exemplary 90+. The breakdown is shown on the profile ("How this is scored"). We don't fact-check sources automatically; "accurate" means sourced and not removed.
- **Source links:** any dispatch can carry one https link (composer link button). Shown as a "Source: domain" chip.
- **Pins:** up to 3 dispatches (was 1). Existing single pins migrate automatically.
- **Profile themes:** six accents from the app palette (electric blue, cyan, violet, emerald, amber, rose) color the banner glow, panel glow, active tab, Credibility gauge and badges on that profile only.
- **Private accounts:** follow becomes a request (notification with Approve/Decline, also listed in Settings → Privacy). Non-followers and logged-out visitors can't see private dispatches anywhere (profile, feeds, search, For You, direct links show "unavailable"), nor follower/following/friend lists. Turning private off approves everyone waiting.
- **Account:** change email (password accounts confirm with their password), notification settings per type (safety notices always arrive), plus the existing sign up/in, edit profile, password, block, mute, report and delete account.
- **Notifications table** was rebuilt (migration 5) to allow the new types: reactions, follow requests and acceptances, achievements.

Each one sends a "Milestone" notification and shows on the profile.

- **Notifications:** the Newsdesk now covers followers, follow requests (Approve/Decline inline), acceptances, mentions, responses, quotes, relays, reactions and milestones, with filter chips and the unread badge in the header. Per-type settings in Settings → Notifications.
- **Rate limits:** follows 15/min and 60/hour; reactions 60/min and 300/hour; dispatches 5/min and 60/hour (unchanged); feed tracking 30/min.
- **Polish pass** (phone 390px and desktop 1440px, dark and light): Vault page renamed and given a friendly empty state with stories to start from; empty Following/Newsdesk show suggestions; page headers unified to the condensed uppercase style; reaction and emoji pop-ups render outside clipping containers (they were cut off on phones); amber and Beat colors deepen in light mode for contrast; feed markup fixed for screen readers (feed items are articles); image buttons' accessible names fixed.

# news-v1: real news, coverage spread, context notes, push alerts

## Part H — Real news intake

- **Sources.** 53 outlets (`src/lib/newsroom/outlets.ts`): 3 wire services, 8 national papers, 9 TV networks, 2 public broadcasters, 22 digital outlets and magazines across left, center and right, 4 international, 5 sports and tech. 51 use the outlet's own official RSS feed. **AP and Reuters publish no public RSS**, so they come only through a licensed news API (`NEWS_API_KEY`, NewsAPI.org format); without the key they are skipped and the Control Room says so. I did not use Google News or any third-party "RSS from any site" service, because those scrape.
- **One feed per outlet.** Each outlet counts once in coverage. More feeds for the same outlet would double-count it, so the Control Room adds a feed as an outlet.
- **What we keep.** Headline (outlet suffixes like " - Fox News" removed), a snippet cut under 280 characters from the feed's own summary, outlet, time, link, and an image URL only if the feed itself provides one (https only). We never read `content:encoded` (full article bodies). Atom `<content>` is used only when there is no `<summary>`, and is cut to the same snippet length. Images are hot-linked with `referrerpolicy="no-referrer"` and credited to the outlet; if one fails to load, the generated art shows instead. No outlet logos: outlets show as a text badge with a small color dot.
- **Politeness.** User-Agent `WorldNetNewsBot/1.0 (RSS reader; headlines and links only; +<APP_URL>/help#news-sources)`, with no personal details. robots.txt is checked before every feed fetch (cached 24 hours; our own group or `*`, longest match wins, Allow wins ties; a missing robots.txt allows, a 5xx means "try later"). Conditional requests (ETag / Last-Modified), so unchanged feeds cost a 304. 15-second timeout, 4 MB cap, 6 fetches at a time. Failures back off exponentially (10 min, 20, 40… up to 6 hours) and honor `Retry-After`. The news API is called every 20 minutes (a 100-calls-a-day plan allows ~72).
- **Feed terms.** Most outlets publish RSS for readers and aggregators, but some feeds' terms limit commercial use. Before a commercial launch, someone should read each outlet's feed terms; any outlet can be paused or removed in the Control Room in one tap.
- **Scheduler.** In-process timer in the web server (`src/instrumentation.ts` → `scheduler.ts`), first run 15 s after start, then every 10 minutes. It works on Railway because the app is one long-running service with SQLite on a volume. A database lock stops overlapping runs. `NEWS_INGEST=off` disables it if you'd rather run `npm run news:ingest` from a Railway cron service. **Run a single replica** (also true before, because of SQLite).
- **Clustering** (`cluster.ts`, pure and unit-tested). Each article is a bag of keywords (lowercase, stopwords removed, light stemming, title words count double), weighted by TF-IDF over the last 72 hours. Articles are processed oldest first; each joins the active story (last article within 36 hours) with the highest cosine similarity if it is ≥ 0.30 and they share at least 2 distinctive title words; otherwise it starts a new story. A merge pass then folds a story created in the same run into a near-duplicate (similarity ≥ 0.33 and 3+ shared distinctive words); only brand-new stories are merged away, so existing story URLs never change. Known limit: two older stories about the same event are not merged later; editors can't merge by hand yet.
- **Neutral headline.** No AI writing. The words that appear in at least 40% of the outlets' headlines are the "common facts". Each headline is scored on how many of those it contains and how few other words, with a small bonus for wire and Center outlets and penalties for opinion pieces, question headlines and loaded words. The winner is then neutralized ("slams/blasts/rips" → "criticizes"; "shocking", "bombshell", exclamation marks and shouting removed). Editors can override a headline (it then stays fixed).
- **Summary.** The story summary is one outlet's snippet (the best-scoring non-opinion article), always shown with "Snippet from <outlet>".
- **Categories** US, World, Politics, Markets, Sports, Tech: keyword scores per article plus a vote from topical feeds (ESPN → Sports). They map onto the app's existing sections (US → the national front page, Markets → Business) so tabs, channels and art keep working.
- **Importance (0–100)** rewards breadth, not clicks: up to 40 for the number of outlets (16+ is full), 20 for coverage from all three sides, 12 for different kinds of outlet, 8 if a wire service has it, 15 for articles in the last 3 hours. Freshness halves every 8 hours. Feeds rank by importance × (0.35 + 0.65 × freshness). Real stories are "developing" until 24 hours without a new article, then "ended"; only editors set "live".
- **Demo data.** The fictional stories and outlets are marked `demo`. They show only while the Control Room switch is on, which is only possible outside production (or with `NEWS_DEMO=1`, for local testing; never on Railway). With demo off, dispatches written about demo stories are hidden from For You and Friends, and the seeded "Breaking" banner (which links to a demo story) is hidden from the ticker. Old demo-story links still open.
- **Retention.** Articles older than 30 days are deleted unless people posted about their story; empty old stories go too. The last 500 sync runs are kept.

## Part I — Coverage spread

- **Left / center / right**: Left + Lean Left, Center, Lean Right + Right. Each outlet counts once per story. Unrated outlets (sports and tech sites) count toward the outlet total but not the shares.
- **Ratings** are approximate summaries of public third-party ratings (AllSides Media Bias Ratings and the Ad Fontes Media Bias Chart, 2024–2025), each with a note saying which, and editable in Control Room → News feeds. Where the two disagree I used AllSides' label and noted Ad Fontes'. Please review them; I am not certain every label matches the raters' latest charts.
- **Flags**, neutral and factual, only with 4+ rated outlets: "Mostly covered by Left/Right-leaning outlets" when one side has 70%+; otherwise "Few Left/Right-leaning outlets covering this" when a side has 10% or less (needs 5+ rated outlets). Center-heavy coverage is never flagged (that's normal for wire-driven news).
- **Colors.** The bar uses a muted blue, gray and muted red, the familiar US convention, and always shows the numbers in text too ("L 40 · C 30 · R 30 · 10 outlets"), so color is never the only cue. This bends the earlier "red only for BREAKING" rule; if you'd rather, I can switch it to three shades of blue.
- **Tap the bar** → a sheet listing outlets grouped by lean (Left … Right, then Not rated), each with its headline, time and a link out to its own article. The story page shows the same list inline under "Who's covering this".

## Part J — Context notes and the bridging score

- **Writing.** Signed-in accounts at least 3 days old (admins exempt), not suspended. 20–280 characters, an http(s) source link required, one note per person per dispatch or story, never on your own dispatch. Limits: 3 per hour, 5 per day.
- **Rating.** Helpful (1), Somewhat helpful (0.5), Not helpful (0), each with a required reason from a fixed list. You can't rate your own note. Limits: 30 ratings per 10 minutes, 200 per day. Ratings from accounts younger than 24 hours are saved but not counted.
- **The bridging score** (`src/lib/notes/bridging.ts`, explained at the top of that file):
  1. Fit `rating ≈ μ + b_rater + b_item + f_rater × f_item` (one-dimensional matrix factorization) over all note ratings **plus agree/disagree stances** on stories and dispatches, so viewpoints are known before there are many notes. Alternating ridge regression, 50 rounds, deterministic. Intercepts are regularized more strongly than factors, so one-sided enthusiasm lands in the f × f term.
  2. Raters with at least 3 ratings and |f| ≥ 0.25 (after scaling to unit spread) join group A (f < 0) or B (f > 0). Others still rate, but don't count toward the cross-group test.
  3. A note is **published** when it has 5+ grouped ratings, 2+ from each group, an average of 60%+ "helpful" in **each** group, and a non-negative intercept. It **stays** published while each group stays at 50%+ (so it doesn't flicker), and **drops** back when that fails. Notes both groups rate at 30% or below become "not helpful" (hidden).
  Many votes from one side never publish a note. Re-scoring runs after every rating (milliseconds at this size).
- **Shown as** "Readers added context" under the dispatch (in feeds too) and on story pages, with the source and a "Why am I seeing this?" page showing each group's count and average. The groups come only from rating patterns; we never label them politically.
- **Abuse.** Anyone can report a note (6 reasons, 10 reports an hour). A note with 3+ open reports is hidden until reviewed. Control Room → Context notes lists reported notes (remove or dismiss), raters with 20+ ratings in the last hour, authors with 3+ removed or rejected notes, and recent status changes.

## Part K — Push alerts

- **Standard web push** with VAPID (`web-push` 3.6.7) and the existing service worker, which now handles `push`, `notificationclick` (focuses an open tab or opens one) and `pushsubscriptionchange`. The site was already an installable PWA (manifest with standalone display and maskable icons).
- **Permission** is requested only when the person flips "Alerts on this device" in Settings → Alerts, never on load. iPhone users are told to add the site to the Home Screen first (iOS only allows web push for installed apps). Private windows never answer the push service; after 20 seconds the switch says so instead of spinning.
- **Types**: breaking news, responses and quotes, mentions, new followers and follow requests, big stories on pinned topics and teams. Each can be turned off. Quiet hours (in the person's own time zone, detected when they turn alerts on; ranges can wrap midnight) **skip** alerts rather than saving them, because news is stale by morning. Breaking alerts are capped per person per rolling 24 hours (default 3, 0–10). Followed-topic alerts are capped at 5 a day and only for stories with importance 55+.
- **Breaking.** Automatic for a story with importance 85+, 8+ outlets and under 6 hours old: at most one per sync and six a day site-wide. Editors can send one from Control Room → Breaking alerts, choosing a story or custom text with a link on this site, with a phone-style preview and a confirm step. **A story can be sent once, ever**: the `breaking_alerts` row is claimed before anything goes out. Every alert is also logged per person (`alert_log`), so nothing repeats.
- **Expired subscriptions.** 404/410 from the push service deletes the subscription; 401/403 (keys changed) too; other failures count up and the subscription is dropped after 5 in a row. A person can have up to 10 devices.
- **Not verified here.** This workspace can't reach browsers' push services, so delivery to a real phone could not be tested; the sender logic is unit-tested against a fake push service. After deploying: Settings → Alerts → turn on → "Send me a test alert".
